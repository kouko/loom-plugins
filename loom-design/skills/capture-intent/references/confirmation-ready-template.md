# Ready confirmation restatement

In the user's plain language, cover what is needed; five optional sections are:
background and current direction; current problem and impact; what changes;
scope and acceptance; and what the user is confirming. Translate any headings.
Connect known, necessary
project or function context to this request. Retain the applicable same-message
authorization and any existing publication hold or opt-out.

Use only supplied or evidenced facts, requirements, boundaries and guarantees;
do not infer unspecified restrictions from goals or examples. For example,
"results may differ" does not mean "the method cannot change"; do not copy
example facts. For context, connect supplied facts:
<person> faces <problem>; this change enables <outcome>.
Preserve supplied ordering and conditions that affect acceptance.
Keep sections proportionate and avoid repetition or filling unknown context.

Use a table for a helpful comparison, or an ASCII flow for a useful or requested
flow comparison; label before and expected after inside each frame and align
its edges. Follow the existing visualization skill when available. Do not force
a diagram for a wording correction. Avoid paths,
identifiers and mechanism names unless needed to understand the request.
