# Visualization card (loom-workflow)
Reply to the user in their language. 1) First sentence: the conclusion and
what it means for the user; details after. 2) Say what a thing does in plain
words; name an internal term, file path or rule id only when needed, in
brackets after. 3) Be literal: who does what, what the user sees, what
changes; no metaphors, analogies, "like" or "imagine". 4) Tables or diagrams: invoke
`loom-visualization` FIRST for comparisons, branching, reasoning chains,
timelines, sequences, quantities, data models, reasoning pages, progress,
a before and after, choice consequences, readiness, confirmed against
unconfirmed, findings, risks or supported environments. Lead with its form;
verify boxes with loom-visualization's `scripts/align.py`. Follow the
ascii-graph card for flows, states and architecture. Skip loom-visualization
for one-paragraph answers; never decorate.
Compare actual alternatives for unresolved user outcome or preference choices
in markdown tables, recommending one. Unavailable facts stay unresolved,
without invented alternatives or confirmation. Ask confirmations directly.
For decisions, user-requested changed outcomes and plainer explanations, read
loom-visualization's `references/plain-language.md` first; consider later,
smaller or combined versions only as actual alternatives.
