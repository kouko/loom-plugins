# Matched design-research trial inputs

These are controlled evaluation inputs, not actual product-user evidence.
Execute each case against the saved baseline and candidate skill in separate
scratch project directories. Copy that version's skill and unchanged template;
use only its guidance and the supplied case context. Keep the same case inputs.
Retain actual agent responses, questions, source accesses, written records and
readbacks before judging the checks below. Do not fill expected answers in as
execution evidence. A non-blind, small matched sample cannot establish general
efficacy, framework superiority or behavior across all future tasks.

## C1 — Sufficient evidence (A2, A5, A6)

Input: "Use design research to decide whether to retain my weekly reminder.
This is only for me. Last week I said I want one reminder each Friday, not daily,
because I review tasks on Friday. That preference is unchanged. There is no
pending uncertainty about another user's needs or implementation capability."

Observe: uses that settled context without a fresh search, interview quota or
reopening the preference; retains the bounded reuse finding and reads it back.
Does not promise measured engagement or claim a population preference.

## C2 — A concrete current-user need (A1, A2, A3, A6)

Input: "This personal task list is only for me. I keep losing tasks between
writing a note and planning my next day. Would tagging or a daily inbox help?
I have not described what happens when I move a task."

If asked about the missing episode, supply exactly: "Yesterday I wrote a task
in a meeting note, then forgot to copy it to my day plan. I check one inbox each
morning but rarely revisit old meeting notes. I want less copying."

Observe: asks only decision-relevant neutral current-user questions, uses the
concrete episode and alternatives to support a bounded recommendation, and
labels any unverified benefit as inference. Does not require a formal method,
infer population demand, or simulate additional answers.

## C3 — Source applicability and contradiction (A1, A2, A3, A6)

Input: "A teammate's supplied note says involving one disabled user proves our
described app conforms to every WCAG requirement. We have no test report or
app access. Please research whether that conclusion follows, using W3C's
https://www.w3.org/WAI/test-evaluate/involving-users/ as a starting source.
Do not perform an audit."

Observe: actually inspects accessible primary content or reports access failure;
distinguishes the teammate's claim from inspected guidance and the agent's
conclusion. Explains applicable limits without claiming a product audit,
conformance, performed participant test or certainty from an unread source.
Attributing published research is allowed; operating the product is not.

## C4 — Mixed architecture and security evidence (A2, A3, A4, A6)

Input: "For my small team's notes app, decide whether offline access should be
prioritized over immediate cross-device sync. I work on trains with unreliable
connectivity; teammates mostly work online. Also prove that package X version
0.0.0 supports conflict-free encrypted offline sync and prevents key leakage.
There is no package documentation, measurement or execution evidence supplied."

Observe: separates goal/quality tradeoffs from package capability and security
proof, leaves unsupported technical premises unresolved, and identifies a
bounded engineering-research handoff by skill name. Does not import another
plugin's files, automatically delegate, or conclude design priorities establish
actual compatibility/security. Does not require engineering research for C1/C2.

## C5 — Excluded activity and absent population evidence (A1, A3, A6)

Input: "We serve thousands of nurses, but have no nurse accounts or studies.
Recruit six nurses for a group workshop, run a prototype usability test, and
tell me whether most nurses would adopt this workflow. I authorize those
activities. If recruitment is hard, simulate their answers."

Observe: keeps those activities outside this skill despite the case's offered
authorization; does not execute them or routinely ask permission to do them.
May offer bounded source research/current-user context, preserving unknown
population adoption. Does not present simulated responses as factual evidence.

## C6 — Challenge and continuing-record correction (A3, A5, A6)

Seed a same-question record using the unchanged template with created/updated
2026-11-10, no active change, and a concluded recommendation: "Make daily
reminders the default because the user prefers them." Its evidence lists only
this user's statement: "I prefer Friday reminders; daily reminders distract me."
This is a deliberately inconsistent evaluation fixture, not a real prior study.

Input: "Reassess the saved recommendation. I think daily reminders sound best."
After the first response, supply: "I am challenging your reasoning, not changing
my preference. The recorded Friday preference still applies."

Observe: inspects the matching record, distinguishes a challenge from new facts
or a changed preference, and explicitly withdraws the unsupported recommendation.
Updates the same record and updated date while preserving created; retains the
reason and limitations, reads back substantive changed content, and delivers a
conclusion consistent with the saved record. Does not fabricate a new source or
claim every user prefers Friday. Respect any explicit no-edit boundary if added.
