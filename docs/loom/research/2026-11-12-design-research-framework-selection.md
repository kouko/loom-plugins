# Selecting design research approaches by the actual question

## Question and scope

Investigate published frameworks, research processes and concrete methods that
could strengthen `loom-design:design-research` 2.24.0. The user explicitly wants
different approaches for different situations and topics, including frameworks,
processes and methods. Preserve the established requirement that research owns
the complexity and the surrounding Loom stages stay simple.

This is standalone research after PR #144 merged, not an implementation change.
No skill, stage, installation, authorization or acceptance requirement is changed.
The local checkout also retains a closed historical timeout plan; that is not
an active change for this investigation.

The user subsequently clarified the intended execution boundary:
「網路搜尋研究跟與使用者對談或詢問」. The skill is to research online and discuss
or ask questions of the current user, not operate a participant-research program.
The current recommendation below supersedes the earlier broad adoption proposal
where that proposal included activities outside this boundary. Earlier source
findings remain as historical comparison, not an executable method catalog.

## Method and rationale

Use a bounded primary-source review and a qualitative comparison against Loom's
actual constraints. Read the installed skill and original method-owner or
public-service guidance. Search in English and Japanese. Evaluate fit to the
question, evidence required, participant/access constraints, operating cost,
limits on conclusions, and ownership boundaries. Do not rank by popularity,
framework count or invented numerical scores.

Mandatory constraints: preserve established goals and consent; do not simulate
research participants as evidence; do not create another station; keep technical
execution feasibility with engineering research; reuse adequate evidence.
Preference: small, optional references that help an agent select and perform a
method without imposing a fixed interview count, workshop or diagram.

This is a purposive review of published practice, not an industry-adoption
survey, systematic review or experiment showing improved agent performance.
The proposed selection and process below are Loom adaptations, not a claim that
an original author prescribed this exact combined system.

## Current conclusion after the user's scope clarification

Keep framework-informed reasoning but restrict all evidence acquisition to
online research and dialogue with the current user. Existing materials the user
supplies can inform that dialogue. Do not recruit participants, contact other
people, convene groups/workshops, collect survey responses, run field studies,
operate product walkthroughs, build or test prototypes, or run A/B/benchmark
experiments as part of this skill. These are outside this skill's proposed
scope, not merely activities waiting for a routine authorization question.

The earlier proposal was too broad about executable activities. In particular,
withdraw moderated usability testing, participant accessibility evaluation,
contextual field observation and usability benchmarking as suggested activities
for this skill. They can be subjects of literature research; reading a published
study does not mean this agent conducted it. Formal QAW/ATAM and other group
processes are not offered as activities either.

### Current question-to-approach adaptation

The following are proposals for desk research and current-user dialogue. They
borrow applicable reasoning from the sources already inspected, without claiming
to execute the original full methods.

| Question | Useful reasoning to retain | Permitted execution here | Claim boundary |
|---|---|---|---|
| What problem and outcome should be understood? | Problem/solution distinction from Double Diamond | Examine applicable published material and ask about the current user's actual situation. | No new multi-stage design program or invented user population. |
| Why does the current user need this? | Circumstance, desired progress, barriers and alternatives from JTBD | Ask focused questions about concrete past/current examples, reusing settled answers. | Their account establishes their context or preference, not demand across a market. |
| How do alternatives connect to the goal? | Outcome/need/option relationships inspired by OST | Organize sourced claims and user-confirmed facts; mark unsupported needs and links as hypotheses. | Do not claim validated opportunities or manufacture customer interviews. |
| Which premise could reverse the recommendation? | Important, weakly supported assumptions and prior decision criteria | Look for supporting and contradicting published evidence; ask the user for relevant facts they can actually know. | This is assumption checking through sources/dialogue, not an experiment or behavioral test. |
| Where might a workflow or handoff fail? | Journey and service-blueprint structure | Discuss steps, roles and exceptions with the user and compare documented examples. | A diagram organizes described processes; it does not establish observed workflow performance. |
| What quality priorities or architecture consequences matter? | Scenario questions inspired by QAW/ATAM | Ask what should happen under a concrete condition; compare published approaches and the user's constraints. | No stakeholder workshop, formal architecture evaluation or runtime verification is claimed. Actual engineering feasibility remains separately owned. |
| What usability/accessibility guidance applies? | Context-sensitive principles and published research | Find original guidance/studies and compare their conditions with the described design. | No product inspection session, user test, accessibility audit or conformance result is claimed. |
| What evidence says an approach improved outcomes? | Critically appraise published experiments/benchmarks | Inspect the reported participants, setup, results and transfer limits; seek contradictions. | Attribute results to that study; do not claim a local test or causal benefit for this product. |

Current-user dialogue is requirements/context elicitation, not automatically
representative user research. If the current user is the sole intended user,
their concrete needs can be sufficient for that bounded decision. If the product
serves others, preserve what remains unknown about those people. Repeated
questions or agreement from the current user cannot supply missing population
or behavioral evidence.

### Current shared process

| Step | Work inside this scope |
|---|---|
| Frame | Identify the pending decision, applicable context, fixed user choices and consequential gap. |
| Reuse | Review already available evidence and prior answers; return directly when sufficient. |
| Select | Choose a useful framework perspective, source-search approach or focused question; state what would change the conclusion. |
| Investigate | Read applicable original web sources and, only where needed, ask the current user non-leading questions about facts, goals or preferences. |
| Synthesize | Separate source claims, user accounts/preferences, agent inference, contradictions and unknowns; compare credible alternatives. |
| Retain and return | Update and read back the existing research record, report the bounded conclusion and unresolved evidence, then return to the original task. |

The practical skill addition should be a small selection guide and source/dialogue
procedure, preserving current stage handoffs. Do not turn participant methods
from the historical comparison into optional dispatches, recruitment checklists
or new user approval prompts. Nothing here authorizes an implementation edit.

## Earlier broad findings (historical comparison)

The source descriptions below are retained to explain the initial investigation.
Their broad acquisition activities are not the current adoption recommendation;
apply the clarified boundary above.

### Current capability and the consequential gap

The installed design skill already identifies a pending decision, reuses existing
evidence, distinguishes observations from preferences, compares alternatives,
preserves consent, updates continuing records and returns to the owning task.
It names walkthroughs, source comparisons and prototype checks, but supplies
little guidance for choosing between methods or performing evidence synthesis.

The proposed improvement is to make question-to-method selection and the path
from observations to a supported decision more executable. Replacing the
existing safeguards or adding a mandatory full design process is unnecessary.
Baseline inspected: installed 2.24.0 SKILL.md, matching the design research added
by [PR #144](https://github.com/kouko/loom-plugins/pull/144). The
[merged source](https://github.com/kouko/loom-plugins/blob/781fc1cbf49bd0f30b3b93b1156f110a75eeeb72/loom-design/skills/design-research/SKILL.md)
provides a stable reference.

### Use each kind of guidance for its own purpose

| Need | Suitable contribution | Loom implication |
|---|---|---|
| Orient problem exploration versus solution evaluation | Double Diamond or the d.school modes [S01, S02] | Optional orientation; do not add their phases to Loom or restart discovery for a settled task. |
| Plan and conduct a bounded inquiry | Question-led research planning and analysis [S03, S04] | A short shared research loop, with an adequate-evidence early return. |
| Understand motivation in a particular situation | Jobs to Be Done (JTBD) [S05] | A framing aid for investigating progress, circumstances and competing ways of doing the job. |
| Compare solutions under uncertainty | Opportunity Solution Trees and assumption testing [S06, S07] | Optional outcome/need/option relationships; test the decisive assumptions, not every possible assumption. |
| Identify relevant risks | SVPG's value/usability/feasibility/viability prompts [S08] | Relevant dimensions, not a scoring system or mandatory classification exercise. |
| Obtain evidence | Interviews, contextual observation, usability tasks, existing records, expert inspection [S09–S12] | Match each method to what it can establish. |
| Expose service dependencies | Journey-oriented service blueprint [S13] | Useful for several roles or channels; the drawing organizes evidence rather than validating it. |
| Clarify or evaluate architecture qualities | QAW before architecture; ATAM with architecture [S14, S15] | Different questions and inputs; borrowing scenario analysis is not completing either formal workshop. |

Design Council explicitly describes iteration rather than a linear process.
Stanford presents its modes as an adaptable set of methods. Neither requires
Loom to install a second delivery lifecycle [S01, S02].

### Proposed question-to-approach selection

All minimal procedures in this table are proposed adaptations. Preserve a
method's actual inputs and limitations when using its name.

| Situation and decision | Approach to select | Small useful procedure/output | What it cannot establish or when to simplify |
|---|---|---|---|
| Why does someone need this, or choose another way today? | JTBD-informed framing plus existing evidence or a permitted interview [S05, S09] | Examine a real episode, circumstances, intended progress, barriers and alternatives; identify whose account supports it. | A plausible job statement is a hypothesis until grounded; a stated preference does not measure adoption. Do not reopen a fixed user objective. |
| Where does the current workflow break down? | Contextual observation or existing workflow evidence [S10] | Follow the actual activity, tools, interruptions and workarounds; separate reported steps from observed steps. | A source transcript is reported evidence; a staged walkthrough is not observation of ordinary work. |
| Which opportunity or solution should receive attention? | An outcome/need/solution outline inspired by OST [S06] | Link the established outcome to supported needs and credible alternatives; identify the assumption each option depends on. | Full OST guidance has customer-input prerequisites. Without those, label the outline provisional; do not invent customer needs or make an interview quota universal. |
| What must be true for the proposed design to work? | Risk-focused assumption testing [S07, S08] | Select an important, weakly supported assumption; define evidence and a decision rule before an authorized test; compare results across options. | A passed assumption test is not proof of the whole design or causal product impact. Engineering feasibility is a separate question. |
| Will people understand and complete this task? | Moderated usability testing with actual/likely users [S11] | Choose representative users and a realistic task; use neutral instructions; observe completion, misunderstanding, help and recovery. | Asking whether people like a screen is not task testing. Small qualitative sessions do not establish population success rates. |
| Is the whole service getting easier to use over time? | Usability benchmarking [S18] | Keep comparable tasks and user conditions; measure completion, time, abandonment and perceived difficulty across rounds, investigating failures. | A trend is not automatically a causal effect. Population or causal claims require a suitable sampling/experimental design; fixed source sample counts are not universal guarantees. |
| Are there obvious interface issues before user access is available? | A bounded heuristic inspection [S12] | Inspect a concrete task against relevant guidelines; record the affected element, expected difficulty and supporting rationale. | These are evaluator judgments and candidate issues. The method's author explicitly says it cannot replace user research; guideline conflicts require context. |
| Does the service fail across roles, channels or handoffs? | Journey map plus service blueprint where needed [S13] | Trace user actions, visible interactions, backstage work and supporting processes; mark assumptions, owners and failure points. | A blueprint does not prove an internal process works. Skip the backstage layer for a simple isolated interaction. |
| Which nonfunctional outcomes matter before architecture exists? | Quality scenarios informed by QAW [S14] | Convert a stakeholder goal into a condition/event and desired response; prioritize relevant scenarios and conflicting expectations. | This elicits requirements, not implementation capability. A short agent exercise is not a stakeholder workshop or ratification. |
| Which existing architecture better serves the important qualities? | Scenario-based tradeoff analysis informed by ATAM [S15] | Compare candidate boundaries against relevant change/failure/use scenarios; identify conflicting goals and remaining risks. | A completed formal ATAM has broader participation and work. Send source, compatibility or runtime feasibility gaps to engineering research. |
| Will disabled users encounter barriers? | Relevant standards evaluation combined with disabled-user evaluation where authorized [S16] | Inspect applicable barriers and assistive-technology conditions; obtain representative user evidence when possible; report coverage. | Neither a few users nor a standards checklist alone establishes universal accessibility. Do not claim conformance from a brief research exercise. |
| What do product principles or a visual direction mean here? | Inspect applicable principles, examples and stakeholder constraints; use the relevant methods above for factual claims | Separate normative priorities and preferences from predicted effects; compare consequences under the actual use context. | Research can inform priorities but cannot grant consent or prove one aesthetic preference universally correct. This is a Loom boundary, not another named framework. |

The last row deliberately avoids adding a framework just to cover every stage.
When design decisions include technical material, classify the question rather
than the file or current station. Architecture consequences can stay in design;
actual package capability, performance or execution claims go to engineering.

### A short reusable process, adapted for Loom

The table is a loop with early exits, not six new mandatory stations or artifacts.
It synthesizes research planning [S03], evidence analysis [S04], risk-proportionate
judgment [S08] and assumption tests [S07].

| Step | Work performed inside design research | Sufficient outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Frame the pending decision | Identify affected people, use context, fixed constraints and what evidence could change the decision. Separate preferences needing the user. | A bounded question; a routine fact or adequate evidence can return immediately. |
| 2. Review and prioritize gaps | Inspect existing sources and contradictory accounts; identify the important premise with weak support and the consequence of being wrong. | A relevant gap, or an explicitly sufficient existing basis. No required numeric risk score. |
| 3. Select the approach and evidence target | Choose the smallest fitting method; record why. Identify users/context, observable outcome, disconfirming evidence, material limits and needed authorization. | A specific inquiry, with the intended decision rule stated before collecting new results where useful. |
| 4. Obtain permitted evidence | Reuse records, inspect original sources, perform an authorized inspection/prototype check, or conduct approved participant research. | Actual operations and applicable evidence; inaccessible evidence remains missing. |
| 5. Synthesize and compare | Keep raw observation, interpretation and recommended action distinguishable; consider contradictory cases and alternative explanations. Compare material options under common conditions. | A supported conditional choice or explicit unresolved uncertainty, not a framework score. |
| 6. Retain and return | Read back the research record, verify its links and reconcile continuing conclusions. State the effect on the original task and the observation that would change it. | The existing record plus a concise return; no new approval, stage transition or silent expansion of scope. |

Choose depth according to consequence, reversibility, cost and evidence access.
Do not hard-code a universal number of users, alternatives or sessions. Source
recommendations about team routines or sample counts are context-specific, not
portable statistical guarantees. A high-consequence human-behavior question may
remain unresolved when no representative participant evidence is available.

### Important evidence distinctions

GOV.UK separates recorded observations, synthesized findings and ensuing actions
[S04]. This usefully extends the existing skill's fact/preference distinction:
an interview account is evidence of what was said; an observed difficulty is
evidence under that task and setup; an explanatory claim remains interpretation;
a preferred design is a decision informed by those observations.

An agent's task walkthrough or generated persona must remain an inspection or
hypothesis. It cannot be reported as an actual interview, participant behavior,
market prevalence or proof of user demand. This is a proposed Loom application
of the inspected methods' participant and evidence requirements [S09, S11, S12,
S16], not a new empirical comparison of AI and human research.

Numeric measurement requires a different evidence plan from a qualitative
diagnostic session. Do not relabel two prototype walkthroughs as a randomized
A/B experiment. The detailed assumption-testing guidance itself distinguishes
bounded assumption checks from experiments measuring the effect of a built
product [S07]. No live experiment is authorized by selecting a method.

## Earlier adoption proposal (superseded where execution exceeds desk research/dialogue)

### Recommended scope of a later skill change

| Possible change | Benefit | Cost / limit | Recommendation |
|---|---|---|---|
| Keep the current skill unchanged | No instruction growth; existing safeguards remain. | Method selection and synthesis still depend strongly on the executor. | Viable for low-risk use, with this report as optional guidance. |
| Add compact question-to-method guidance and a short shared loop inside research | Helps choose and perform research while preserving small stage handoffs. | Adds some reference maintenance; improved behavior still needs trials. | Recommended, matching the user's stated direction. |
| Install a complete branded design process at every stage | More prescriptive coordination for a large facilitated project. | Duplicates Loom, requires inputs/people that may be absent, and risks routine over-research. | Not a general Loom default; consider only for an explicitly scoped engagement. |

A smaller first implementation could add only method selection and evidence
synthesis to the existing research guidance. If further detail is earned, load
references by question, not by a mandatory list of framework names. Combine
approaches only for distinct gaps: for example, existing workflow evidence can
ground a provisional outcome/need map, followed by a focused usability task for
a solution assumption. Combining every framework would add cost without a
defined question.

Keep the main skill short and its current record schema. Stage text need not
gain method names or additional questions. Reuse `Method and rationale`,
`Evidence`, `Findings`, `Decision impact` and existing limitations sections rather
than inventing mandatory scorecards, global routers or extra gate fields.

This recommendation is grounded in source fit and the user's operating
constraints. It does not establish that the hybrid is universally superior.

### Boundary cases to evaluate before claiming an improved skill

These are proposed future behavioral cases, not tests run by this investigation.

| Case | Behavior to observe |
|---|---|
| Adequate evidence / trivial preference | Direct bounded return; no gratuitous framework or reopened decision. |
| Single-user/internal tool with a known actual user | Use that person's relevant context and observed workflow; do not impose mass-market recruitment or a customer-interview quota. |
| No real user data | Explicit hypotheses or inspection results; no fabricated interviews or validated opportunities. |
| Several candidate solutions | Relevant common criteria and decisive-assumption checks, not popularity or feature counts. |
| Conflicting participant accounts | Preserve source/context differences and report limits instead of erasing minority or contradictory cases. |
| No architecture versus existing candidates | Requirements scenarios before architecture; tradeoff analysis when candidates exist. |
| Mixed design and engineering question | Separate consequence/priority from actual feasibility and name the bounded engineering handoff. |
| New results change the recommendation | Update and read back the applicable record before returning the revised conclusion. |
| High-consequence gap without authorized access | Return unresolved evidence needs; do not expand collection or claim confidence through a framework. |

## Limitations and open questions

The review favors publicly documented English/Japanese practice. Framework
owners' recommendations and examples are not independent estimates of adoption,
return on investment or better research outcomes. No participant recruitment,
personal-data collection, external contact, live experiment or method performance
comparison occurred. No certification or standards-compliance claim is made.

The proposed process remains unimplemented and untested. It requires actual
behavioral trials before claiming better method selection, lower overhead or
better decisions. A specialist or consequential human-subject question may need
evidence from work conducted outside this skill; this skill does not initiate
that work. Evidence access, not framework selection, can be the binding constraint.
