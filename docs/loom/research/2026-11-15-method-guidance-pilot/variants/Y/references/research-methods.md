# Research methods

Choose the smallest investigation that resolves a decision-changing unknown. Reuse sufficient applicable evidence; otherwise inspect sources, diagnose, compare alternatives, or measure as the question requires. Combine methods only for distinct consequential gaps. Explain why the chosen evidence can answer the question and stop when it does.

For a comparison or experiment, state the hypothesis, baseline, what changes, what stays fixed, success and guardrail measures, and limitations before execution. Match inputs and configurations, account for variability and order/cache effects, and do not treat development cases as independent population evidence. Preserve failed or unavailable observations. A prototype is not production readiness and does not authorize implementation.

Do not install software, contact others, or send project information outside the authorized scope. Source acceptance, authorization, retained records, verification, and evaluation-selection requirements remain unchanged.
